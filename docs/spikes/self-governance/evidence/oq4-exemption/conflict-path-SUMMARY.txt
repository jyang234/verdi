exemption's review window today, through `verdi context conflict`
(the doc comment on cmd/verdi/context_conflict.go:207 names build start,
gate, and close as the same factory's other callers; readiness_snapshot.go

an exemption's review window does not work end to end today" — that
sentence was proven true for `verdi lint`/`verdi journey` only, and this
file's transcripts prove the opposite is true on the conflict path.

  → the claim IS excused.

Lapsed window (expiry 2026-01-01, exemption-lapsed-window.md): after

  exit: 1

  removed_claims: []

  Full report: conflict-lapsed-window-report.json (483 lines pretty-printed)

`diff` between the two full reports (command: `diff future-window-report
lapsed-window-report`, both pretty-printed with `python3 -m json.tool` in
this directory) shows two kinds of change, and only two:

- Meaningful, exactly where F2 said to look: `resolution.bound` (proven
  vs. violated-with-witness), `removed_claims` (populated vs. empty), and
  — one level up, a consequence of removal this spike had not
  anticipated but is exactly what "removed" should mean — the mechanical
  domain proof itself: `open_domain` flips true (future: the claim is
  gone, so the "golangci-lint-linter-set" subject is unconstrained) vs.
  false (lapsed: the claim's own required value is still the active
  constraint), and `values`/`witnesses` go from `[]`/`[]` to
  `["standard"]`/`["standard"]` to match.
- Incidental content-address churn: the report's own top-level `digest`,
  `effective_policy_digest`, the exact commit `value` named under
  `repository.head`, `manifest_digest`, and the judge's `input_digest`/
  `id` all differ — expected and uninteresting, since every one of them
  is a canonical hash over content that includes the edited `expiry`
  byte, not a sign that anything else in the evaluation changed.

Nothing else differs: `mechanical[0].state` ("proven" both times —
neither run's underlying fact was ever actually violated, only whether
the exemption stood between it and the evaluation), `verdict`,
`semantic`, and `disclosures` are byte-identical. This is the isolated,
single-variable proof review finding F2 asked for: resolveBound(e.Expiry,
...) really does flip between ProofProven and ProofViolatedWithWitness by
calendar date, and allProven really does gate whether the claim is
removed — and, one level up, whether the domain the mechanical evaluator
reasons over is open or still constrained — on the real `verdi context
conflict` path, through a real store.

## What this does NOT show

Both runs' overall `verdict` is "blocked-unproven" — unrelated to this
exemption entirely. `spec/self-governance-spike`'s own prose (co-1..co-4,
dc-1..dc-3, five open questions, problem/outcome) all register as
semantic candidates needing a disposition; the fake judge's "no-conflict"
finding is not itself a disposition, so those candidates stay "unproven"
regardless of the window. A target with no semantic candidates at all
(a minimal fixture spec, the way cmd/verdi/testdata/localoperator/ builds
one) would very likely reach verdict "pass" for the future window and
something else for the lapsed one — not attempted here; this spike's
mechanical-block-level comparison above is sufficient to answer oq-4
without adding that extra fixture-authoring step within the timebox.

`verdi lint` and `verdi journey --json` remain byte-identical between the
lapsed and future windows (unaffected by anything above — see the
lapsed-/future-lint.stdout and lapsed-/future-journey.stdout files in this
same directory, produced before this fix round and not touched by it).
